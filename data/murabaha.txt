Definition:
Murabaha is an Islamic finance contract based on cost-plus sale. The seller purchases an asset and then sells it to the customer at a known profit margin.

Key Principles:
Murabaha must involve a real asset. The seller must own the asset before selling it. The customer must know the original cost and the profit amount.

Conditions:
The asset must be halal. The price and payment schedule must be agreed upon clearly. Ownership must transfer properly before resale.

Example Scenario:
A bank buys a car for $20,000 and sells it to the customer for $22,000 payable over 24 months.

Comparison with Conventional Finance:
Murabaha differs from an interest-based loan because it is based on trade and asset ownership rather than lending money with interest.

Comparison with Ijara:
Murabaha is a sale contract while Ijara is a lease contract. In Murabaha, ownership transfers to the customer after sale.

Risks or Compliance Issues:
If the bank sells the asset before owning it, the contract may not be Shariah compliant.

Common Misconceptions:
Some people think Murabaha is identical to a conventional loan. However, the structure and legal basis are different.

Important Notes:
Transparency is important in Murabaha. The customer should know the cost price and the profit margin.
